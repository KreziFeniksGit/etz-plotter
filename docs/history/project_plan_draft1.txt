 1. How to read and correct this plan
 This is a first draft built from what is known about the design. Where something is a guess, it says so and points to a
 decision in section 4. Your corrections matter most in four places: the real competition task (D10) and the date, the
 power source (D1), the tools and printer you own (D5, D6), and the amount of time you really have each week.

 Assumptions made while writing
  • Start: Monday 12 October 2026, the first working Monday after today. The competition date is unknown, so the
    plan is 14 weeks with a buffer week at the end. If the real date is earlier, section 3.3 says what to cut and what
    never to cut.
  • Working pattern: five working sessions per week of 3 to 4 hours (the tasks have day names but the days can
    move). Printing, curing and shipping happen while you do something else.
  • Task (assumed, D10): the arm picks small ferrous objects from a flat field with the electromagnet, guided by the
    camera, and places them in zones, operated from a laptop. All timing and scoring work in weeks 10 to 12 follows
    from this assumption.
  • Printer: a machine with a bed of at least 220 x 220 mm that you can use freely; filament PETG; 0.4 mm nozzle.
  • Money: all prices in section 8 are my rough estimates in EUR, not looked up. Replace them with real quotes in
    week 1.
  • Everything electronic follows bare_electronics_pack.pdf (no enclosure, a bare perfboard on four standoffs);
    accuracy and tests follow accuracy_and_test_plan.pdf; printing follows print_guide_and_changes.pdf.

 Task IDs and conventions
 Tasks are numbered week.number (for example 4.3 is the third task of week 4). "Done when" is a test that someone
 else could check. D1 to D12 are decisions, R1 to R20 are risks, T1 to T10 are the physical tests of the accuracy
 plan, C1 to C5 are the calibrations. File names such as hardware_bom.csv refer to the v3 deliverable folder.

 2. Where the design stands today
 The mechanical design is complete as a model and has been checked for collisions, fastener engagement, thin
 walls and stiffness. Nothing has been printed or built yet. The numbers below are from the models and the analysis,
 not from measurements, and weeks 8 and 9 exist to replace them with measurements.

  Item                         Value (source)
  Kinematics                   SCARA, L1 = L2 = 150 mm, 300 mm reach, elbow-minus configuration only. Workspace: J1 -0.9 to 116.3
                               degrees, J2 -150.1 to -63.7 degrees (reach study). Mechanical stops: J1 about -3 and 121 degrees; J2
                               +140 and -158 degrees.
  Z axis                       Tr8x2 lead screw 200 mm, rods 220 mm, LM8UU bearings, magnet height 5 to 90 mm (85 mm stroke),
                               about 10 to 15 mm/s at 16 V.
  Drives                       J1 and J2: NEMA17 40 mm with encoder, 20T:60T GT2 belts (3:1); wrist: NEMA11 with encoder; Z:
                               NEMA17 pancake 21.5 mm, open loop, home switch. Four TMC2209 drivers, ESP32-S3.
  Supply                       Maximum 16 V. J1 at 16 V and 90 degrees extended: stall at about 0.51 s; use 0.9 s moves with smoothed
                               ramps (margin 2.76). Peak power about 43 W.

                               percent). Camera 8 MP on a 155 mm boom.
  Printed parts                15 designs, 19 pieces, about 330 g of PETG, no supports. Largest: Z carriage 130 x 128 x 60 mm (about 9
                               hours).

 Known open items from the design review
  • Mast to base plate and boom to mast fixings are not modelled (task 1.3 closes this).

  • NEMA11 M2.5 screws engage only 2.0 mm; the wall at two holes is 1.3 mm (use thread-lock, hand tight).
  • Link channels contain many overlapping solids in the STEP; the slicer must merge them (task 2.1 tests it).
  • The J1 stop leaves only about 2 degrees of margin at the low end (check on the first print).
  • Full sequence-aware screw reach was not rerun after the 50 mm Z extension.
  • Rod bending over the longer span (estimated 0.1 mm) is not in the droop figure.
  • The rule wording on the charged voltage (16.8 V) of a 4S pack is not yet read (task 1.1, decision D1).

 3.3 What to cut if time is short
 If the real date is earlier, remove in this order: (1) buffer week 14, (2) the speed optimisation (task 11.4) and the
 second half of the spares work, (3) UI polish (task 10.4) and the poster, (4) the extra tests T6, T8 and T9. Never
 remove: the tolerance plates and test prints of weeks 1 and 2, the pre-power checklist, the calibration C1 to C5, test
 T10 (torque margin), the soak test, or at least one outside rehearsal. If the date is earlier than 10 weeks away, tell
 me and I will rebuild the plan with parallel tracks and fewer iterations.

 3.4 Minimum viable demo
 If only weeks 1 to 8 happen, you still have an arm that homes itself, moves to a point clicked on the camera image
 after the 4-fiducial calibration, picks and places one object at a time under operator supervision, and is documented
 by test data. This is the fallback in risk R20. Everything later makes it faster, more automatic and better presented.

 3.5 Critical path
 The longest chain is: rules and decisions (week 1) to orders (week 1) to deliveries of motors and the plate (weeks 2
 to 3) to the big prints (weeks 3 to 4) to the Z column (week 4) to the shoulder, links and wrist (weeks 5 to 6) to first
 motion (week 6) to calibration (week 8). Firmware and app work fill the waiting time and are only critical from week
 7. The plate and the motors are the likeliest delays, which is why they are ordered first.

 Belt tension
 No belt tension figure has been calculated for this design, so find it by experiment in week 5. Try three tensions on
 each belt: just taut, slightly tighter, clearly tighter. For each, turn the joint by hand through its range (drag should stay
 light) and run a fast reversing move of 10 repetitions (no skipped teeth, which shows as an offset at the encoder).
 Pick the lowest tension that never skips, write the pluck pitch from a phone tuner app and the motor slot position in
 the build log, and use those as the target for every later check. Retighten after the first hour of running, because
 PETG settles.

 Torque values
 M3 screws into Ruthex inserts: snug plus a quarter turn, never more. M3 into the metal clamps: moderate. M2.5 in
 the NEMA11 tapped holes: very light, thread-lock, because only 2 mm of thread engages. Grub screws on the shaft
 flats: tighten, check after 10 minutes of running, add thread-lock.

 Things that must not be forgotten
 Cable clips on link 1 go on before covers. Belts go on before link 2. The wrist bracket bolts to link 2 before the link 2
 cover goes on. Keep the Z carriage above 16 mm when the arm is folded over the column (parked pose warning).
 Remove the shipping tape from the camera lens.

 6.3 Electronics build and bring-up
 Follows bare_electronics_pack.pdf. Maximum supply 16 V. TMC2209 modules accept 4.75 to 29 V, the LM2596
 steps down to 5 V for the ESP32, the 12 V magnet is driven by a logic-level MOSFET with a flyback diode and
 PWM. Peak power is about 43 W (2.7 A at 16 V).

  Signal                                    ESP32-S3 pin              Note
  STEP J1, J2, Z, wrist                     4, 5, 6, 7
  DIR J1, J2, Z, wrist                      15, 16, 17, 18
  ENABLE (shared)                           8                         Active low; also pulled high by a resistor so the motors are off while
                                                                      the ESP32 boots.
  TMC2209 UART (shared, one wire)           9, 10                     Four addresses on one bus (MS1/MS2 pins).
  Encoder J1 A, B                           1, 2                      PCNT
  Encoder J2 A, B                           41, 42                    PCNT
  Encoder wrist A, B                        38, 39                    PCNT
  Z home switch                             40                        Normally closed, pull-up.
  Magnet gate (MOSFET)                      47                        PWM; 100 ohm series, 10 k pull-down.


 Build order
 1. Passive board without chips: power rail, fuse, E-stop terminals, capacitors, connectors. Test with a meter. 2. Add
 the LM2596 and check the 5 V output before the ESP32 is plugged in. 3. Add the ESP32 and the drivers one by
 one, testing each with a single motor at low current. 4. Add the MOSFET stage last, test with a resistive load, then
 the magnet. 5. Connect encoders, check counts by hand. Do not plug or unplug a motor while the rail is powered: it
 can destroy the driver.

 Driver current
 Start every motor at about half its rated current (see the datasheet of the motors you actually receive), raise only
 when the torque is not enough, and watch the motor temperature (touch test at 60 C and stop). The UART lets the
 firmware reduce the hold current when the arm is idle.

 Pre-power checklist (appendix C) and the first-power procedure
 Current-limited bench supply at 12 V and 1 A for the first minute, then 2 A, then the pack. Check for heating with a
 finger after 30 seconds. Use a power strip with a switch for the laptop and the supply so that everything can be
 turned off with one hand.

 Rates to keep in mind: with 16 microsteps and the 3:1 belt the joints have 9600 steps per revolution (0.0375 degrees
 per step); the Z screw has 1600 steps per mm at lead 2 mm. At 15 mm/s the Z axis therefore needs 24 kHz of step
 pulses, comfortably within reach of the ESP32-S3. Keep interrupt work short, keep floating-point work out of the step
 interrupt, and log with a timestamp on every fault.
 Version every firmware build with a git hash printed at start-up and stored with each test log. Do not change
 firmware between tests T1 to T4 and their comparison.

 Appendix A. Printed part QC checklist
 Copy one line per part into the log, or print this page.
 ■ Part name and print date written on the part.
 ■ No warp, layer shift or elephant foot that stops the part from sitting flat.
 ■ All holes pass a drill bit or pin gauge test at the model diameter (screws 3.4, bearings 22.0 to 22.4, rods 8.1 to

 ■ 608 seats: bearing presses in by hand pressure or with a light tap; no cracks around the seat.
 ■ LM8UU bores: bearing is held without play, rolls freely.
 ■ Clamp hubs: slit is open and clean; test with the d8 or d5 shaft.
 ■ Bridges and overhangs are clean; no strings across openings.
 ■ Mass within 10 percent of the table.

 Appendix B. Incoming inspection of delivered parts
 ■ Count items against the order and the BOM.
 ■ Motors: shaft diameter, shaft flat, cable length, connector type, rotate by hand, check resistance of both windings
 (should be equal).
 ■ Encoder motors: read the encoder lines and voltage on the label; confirm A and B outputs.
 ■ Pulleys: bore and tooth count, grub screw threads.
 ■ Rods: diameter with calipers at three points, straightness on a flat surface.
 ■ Lead screw: straightness (roll on a flat surface), nut moves smoothly.
 ■ Bearings 608ZZ: rotate by hand, no roughness.
 ■ Belts: length (count teeth), no cracks.
 ■ Electromagnet: resistance, voltage rating on the label (must be 12 V), polarity marking.
 ■ Boards: no bent pins or solder bridges; ESP32 enumerates on USB.
 ■ Photograph the labels and keep the supplier order number next to each box.

 Appendix C. Pre-power checklist
 ■ No bare wire or loose strand, all crimps tugged by hand.
 ■ Polarity of the supply, the buck, every driver and the magnet checked with the meter in continuity mode.
 ■ No short between the supply rail and ground (should read open or a high resistance).
 ■ Fuse of the correct rating (6 A), E-stop is normally closed and cuts the motor rail.
 ■ Capacitors have the correct polarity and voltage rating (35 V or more).
 ■ Every driver has its jumpers/addresses set as on the pin table.
 ■ Motors not connected on the very first power-up; connect them one at a time.
 ■ Current limit on the bench supply set to 1 A.
 ■ Metal objects removed from the board area.
 ■ Someone knows where the power switch is.
